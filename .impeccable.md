# Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Hiring managers and recruiters at big tech companies (FAANG-tier targets). They're browsing during work hours, likely on desktop, evaluating dozens of candidates. They need to quickly feel: "this person is sharp, collaborative, and easy to work with." Secondary audience: engineering/design leaders who care about systems thinking.

### Brand Personality
**3 words: grounded, bright, precise**

Debbie is a Senior Front-End Engineer who loves the outdoors and brings that same curiosity and warmth to her work. She's easy-going and collaborative — someone you'd want to both work with AND go hiking with. But underneath that warmth is serious technical depth: systems thinking, data visualization, accessibility expertise, HCI background.

The tension to resolve: *approachable and human* vs. *technically rigorous*. The answer isn't to split them — it's to show they're the same thing.

### Aesthetic Direction
**"Forest Systems"** — the idea that a great front-end engineer sees the web the way an ecologist sees a forest: layered systems, interconnected dependencies, emergent behavior from elegant rules. Not cold and mechanical. Not cute and rustic. Both at once.

**Visual tone**: Editorial field guide meets engineering documentation. Warm cream backgrounds, forest greens, the existing teal accent (#3DCAAE). Organic but precise. Bright without being neon. Natural without being rustic.

**Theme**: Light mode. FAANG hiring managers browse during the day. The warm, bright palette reads as confident and approachable — not the defensive dark-mode tech bro aesthetic.

**Anti-references**:
- Cold dark-mode developer portfolio (too intimidating, impersonal)
- Pure corporate LinkedIn-style (no personality)
- Over-the-top creative agency maximalism (not legible enough for busy reviewers)
- Generic AI slop: gradient text, glassmorphism, same-card grids, cyan-on-dark

**References to feel**: A premium Scandinavian nature magazine. A well-loved trail guide that's also beautiful. Stripe's documentation but if Stripe loved hiking.

### Typography
- **Display/headings**: Gloock — contemporary editorial serif, warm and slightly playful at large sizes, professional at smaller ones. NOT in the reflex-font banned list. Pairs the "forest" warmth with editorial precision.
- **Body/UI**: Onest — clean humanist sans, warm and readable without being cute or corporate. Balances Gloock's personality with legibility.

### Color System
OKLCH throughout. Forest green + teal brand color + warm cream.
- Background: warm cream tint (not pure white)
- Accent: Teal #3DCAAE (keep as-is, it reads as forest creek / clean water)
- Forest green: deep moss for section accents
- Neutrals: tinted toward the teal/green hue for cohesion

### Design Principles
1. **Warmth before credentials** — Lead with personality, let the work prove the skills
2. **Precision in the details** — Every spacing choice, every typographic decision should feel intentional
3. **Consistent systems, distinctive moments** — Case studies share a unified visual language; individual projects have their own accent personality
4. **Joyful interaction** — Hover states, transitions, and micro-interactions should feel delightful, not performative
5. **Nature as metaphor** — Organic forms (curves, layering, breathing space) grounded in geometric precision
